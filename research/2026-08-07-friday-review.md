## PART 1 — TONIGHT'S FIVE STUDIES

### 1. Rotation-ignition backtest — the strategy FAILED its test, said plainly

**The question:** when the rotation radar promotes a sector to "igniting," does
buying that sector then beat the market over the following days? (This is the
pattern behind today's GDX call purchase.)

**What the data said:** across 116 fresh ignitions in 26 sector funds over two
years, it does not. Three days later the average sector was 0.4 points BEHIND
the market; five days later, still behind; ten days later, still behind. Win
rates 42-51% — coin flips. Worst of all: the STRONGEST ignitions (the +12s and
up — today's miners printed +21) did the worst, losing to the market by 1.3
points in a week with only a 35% win rate.

**What it means for money:** the ignition label, by itself, is not a buy
signal. Sectors that just accelerated hard tend to rest. The GDX position is
NOT invalidated — it has its own exits (profit rungs, the 5-session clock,
the radar-drop rule) and closed green on day one — but the LANE does not get
promoted, and no future trade gets taken on an ignition label alone.

**Limits:** ETF-level entries at the transition close; single-name entries
inside igniting sectors were not tested (that pairing — ignition + the
winner-traits below — is the next question worth asking).

### 3. Tab-lifecycle study (Omar's) — a validated SELLOFF signal, and a one-day pop

**The question:** what does price do after a name lands on each scanner tab —
and is there a few-day option trade in it, long or short?

**What the data said (645 recommendation-days, July-August):**

- **Fresh COOLING arrivals underperform at EVERY horizon** — behind the
  small-cap index by 0.7 points next day, 1.4 by five sessions, 3.2 by ten —
  with every interval excluding zero, on 245 events. This is the most robust
  signal in the study: **a fresh Cooling arrival is a measurable short/put
  candidate.**
- **BUY ZONE arrivals pop +1.2 points the NEXT DAY** (real, interval excludes
  zero) — then give it all back and more, −4.2 by ten sessions. A one-day
  long scalp that turns into a fade.
- The COOLING→BUY ZONE promotion (the scanner's own designed entry): a
  borderline next-day pop, then a HARD fade (−6.4 at ten sessions, n=25).
- The coil "firing" into Fresh Ignition: buying the fire day LOSES (−1.0 next
  day) — chase confirmation, again.
- Honest data limit: the log records recommendation EVENTS, not daily tab
  residency, so true persistence ("stayed on the tab N days") could not be
  measured. Arrivals and transitions are solid; persistence needs the daily
  xlsx snapshots if we want it.

**What it means for money:** two paper-lane candidates, each needing its own
pre-registration before a dollar follows: (a) short-side expression on fresh
Cooling arrivals (puts, few-day hold); (b) the one-day Buy-Zone-arrival pop.
Both fit Omar's "few days, in and out" concept — on the SHORT side more
credibly than the long.
